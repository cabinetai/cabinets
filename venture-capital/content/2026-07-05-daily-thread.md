# Daily X Thread — 2026-07-05

**Topic:** Founders raising now optimize the round on the table when the only thing that round is *for* is setting up the next one. Design the raise backwards — name the milestone that re-rates you, take the least dilution that clears it with margin, and treat the headline valuation as an output of that design, not the input. Capital efficiency isn't austerity; it's optionality.
**Why timely:** This is the vertical-neutral palate cleanser the backlog rotation note calls for after the 07-04 infra thread — angle #1, the strongest queued. All three verticals are now evenly represented in the recent feed (06-20 applied, 06-24 security, 06-28 applied, 07-04 infra), so a craft piece keeps the account from re-stacking any vertical while reading as a fund with a view on the whole game, not just one layer. The take maps to the fund's hard index on efficient growth and the entry-math discipline we apply to every top-of-band deal — framed entirely as a universal round-design principle. No deal, no valuation, no name.

---

**1/** Founders raising right now are optimizing the wrong round. The one on the table isn't the prize — it's the setup. The only job of this round is to make the *next* one an up-round you control. Most term sheets get signed without anyone doing that math.

**2/** Start from the milestone that re-rates you — not from "18 months of runway." Every company has one proof point that flips the next-round conversation from "convince me" to "let me in": an ARR threshold, a retention curve that holds, a technical result the market prices. Name it precisely. That's what this round is *for*.

**3/** Then design backwards: the least dilution that still buys enough runway to clear that milestone *with margin*. Notice what's missing from that sentence — the headline valuation. Price is an output of the design, not the input. Most founders negotiate price first, then discover the round doesn't work.

**4/** Because today's valuation is tomorrow's hurdle. Raise at a number your milestone can't justify and you didn't win the negotiation — you pre-committed to a down round, a flat bridge, or a growth story you'll have to torture the numbers to tell. The highest price is only a win if the next round clears it.

**5/** This is what capital efficiency actually means in this market. Not austerity — optionality. The company that hits its re-rating milestone with nine months of runway left dictates terms. The one that hits it out of cash takes what's offered. Same product, same milestone, opposite negotiations.

**6/** The sentence we listen for in a pitch: "this is the last dilutive round before ___" — with a credible plan for the blank. A founder who can finish it is designing a cap table. A founder who can't is renting time and hoping. The blank is the whole strategy.

**7/** So pitch the round as a designed object: "We're raising $X because milestone M re-rates us, and this dilution leaves enough of the company that the next two rounds are still worth winning." That founder reads as someone who'll still control their company at Series C — and that's who gets clean terms.

We underwrite raises like this every week — the round designed to make the next one inevitable. If that's how you're thinking about yours, our DMs are open.
