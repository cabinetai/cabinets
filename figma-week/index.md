# Figma Week

Every Monday, what changed in your Figma files last week.

## What you get

One page, waiting for you at 9 on Monday morning. Every change in the design files you can
open gets one line — the file, the page inside it, what kind of thing moved, and then the
thing you came for: what the change actually does, in plain words.

The order is the point. A shared component or a variable is used in a hundred places, so
changing one changes all of them; a tweak to a single screen changes that screen and
nothing else. The changes that spread sit at the top, whether or not they happened last.
That is why this is not a list of recent edits: newest first would bury the one change that
breaks your build under six that cannot touch anything.

The routine only reads. Figma can create files, turn a design into code and draw FigJam
diagrams — the Monday run does none of that, on purpose.

## How to look at it

Open the **Week in Design** app in this cabinet. It comes pre-filled with a made-up example
so you can see the shape of it straight away — the page says clearly that those files are
fake. Your first real Monday takes its place. Some Mondays it will be short, or empty; a
quiet week is a real answer, not a broken page.

## Before it can read your real files

Connect Figma in Cabinet's integrations screen: sign in with Figma, approve access, done —
two clicks and nothing to paste. Until you connect, the page keeps the example.

## What's inside

- **Design Watcher** — the one agent. Reads last week's changes, writes the page.
- **Weekly Figma Changes** — the one routine. Runs at 09:00 every Monday.
- **Week in Design** — the one page. Opens on the latest week, with a dropdown for earlier ones.
