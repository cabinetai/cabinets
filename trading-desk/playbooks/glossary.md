# Glossary

| Word | In plain language |
|---|---|
| Long | Buying first, to sell higher later. You gain if the price rises |
| Short | Selling borrowed shares first, to buy back lower. You gain if the price falls |
| Entry, stop, target | Where you get in, where you get out because you were wrong, where you take profit |
| R | Your planned loss on a trade. A win of 2R made twice what you risked |
| Candle | One period of trading as a bar: where it opened, the high, the low, where it closed |
| Volume | How many shares or coins changed hands |
| Relative volume | Today's volume against the usual. 2 means twice as busy |
| Moving average | The average close of the last N days: a line that shows the trend |
| VWAP | The average price paid today, weighted by volume. Day traders treat it as fair price |
| RSI | A 0 to 100 score of how hard a price was bought or sold lately |
| ATR | The usual size of one day's move, in price |
| Support, resistance | A price that acted as a floor, or as a ceiling |
| Gap | An open far from the previous close |
| Float | The shares available for the public to trade |
| Catalyst | The news behind a move |
| Pre-market, after hours | Trading before 9:30 and after 16:00 New York time, on thin volume |
| Earnings | A company's quarterly results. Prices often jump on the report |
| FOMC, CPI | The US central bank's rate decision, and the monthly inflation report |
| VIX | How much movement option prices expect in the S&P 500. High means nervous |
| Short interest | How many shares are sold short. Published twice a month |
| Days to cover | Short interest divided by a normal day's volume |
| Short squeeze | Rising prices force short sellers to buy back, which pushes prices higher |
| Margin | Money borrowed from the broker. It makes gains and losses bigger |
| Option | A contract to buy (call) or sell (put) 100 shares at a set price by a set date |
| 0DTE | An option that expires today. Cheap, fast, and usually worth nothing by the close |
| Implied volatility | The size of move that option prices expect |
| Drawdown | A fall from a high point of your account to a later low |
| Expectancy | What your average trade makes, in R |
| Profit factor | Money won divided by money lost |
