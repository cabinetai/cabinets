# Risk rules

Most traders who lose do not lose because of bad ideas. They lose because one trade was too
big, a stop was moved, or a bad morning turned into a worse afternoon. These rules are the
ones the desk enforces.

## One trade

- **Risk per trade.** Choose the most you will lose on one trade, as a share of the account.
  Many traders use 1% or less. The desk calls that amount **1R**.
- **Size comes from the stop.** Size = (account × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop). A wide stop means
  a small position. A tight stop means a bigger one. The loss is the same either way.
- **A plan has three prices.** Entry, stop, target. Without a stop there is no size and no
  way to measure the result.
- **Reward to risk.** Target distance ÷ stop distance. At 2 to 1 you can be wrong two times
  in three and still break even. Breakeven win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward to risk).
- **A stop inside the noise is not a stop.** If the stop is closer than half the chart's
  usual daily move, an ordinary day will hit it.

## One day

- **Daily loss limit.** Stop trading for the day after losing a set number of R. The desk
  uses 3R unless you change it. It warns you at two losses in a row.
- **No trade to win it back.** A trade taken minutes after a loss, without a plan, is the
  most expensive kind there is.

## Shorts

A short gains when the price falls. Its loss has no ceiling, because a price can keep
rising. Your broker must have shares to lend, may charge a daily fee, and can ask for them
back. A stock that many people are short can jump when they all buy back at once (a short
squeeze). The stop goes above the entry.

## Measuring yourself

| Number | How it is worked out | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| R of a trade | Result ÷ planned risk of that trade | +2R made twice what you risked. A trade sized too big still reads -1R, so also compare its loss with 1R of the account |
| Win rate | Wins ÷ closed trades | Means little without the next two |
| Average trade | Mean R of all trades | Above zero, your method makes money over many trades |
| Profit factor | Money won ÷ money lost | Above 1 is profitable, above 1.5 is good |
| Deepest fall | Largest drop from a high of the account to a later low | How bad it got |

Fewer than 20 closed trades is too few to trust any of them.

## Things worth knowing in the US

- **Day trading rule.** The old rule that needed $25,000 to day trade was removed in 2026.
  Brokers have until October 2027 to switch, so yours may still apply it. Ask them.
- **Wash sales.** A loss on a stock or option does not count for tax if you buy the same
  thing again within 30 days before or after. Crypto is not covered today.
