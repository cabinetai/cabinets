# Sony Network Walkman

> The company that invented portable music, trying to take it back — with the wrong codec.

**Homepage:** sony.com · **Key line:** NW-HD1 $399 (20GB) · NW-HD3 announced for December · Connect store · **Tracked surfaces:** positioning · pricing · product · news · retail

## Who they are

Sony owned portable audio for 25 years, and the NW-HD1 (July 2004) is its first serious hard-disk answer to the iPod: smaller than the iPod, a 30-hour battery, beautiful hardware. But it plays only **ATRAC** — MP3s must be transcoded through the widely disliked SonicStage software — and the **Connect** store (May 2004) is ATRAC-only too. The brand is huge; the ecosystem is fighting itself.

- **Scale:** the biggest consumer-electronics brand on the shelf; Connect store live in US/EU; Walkman brand relaunch under way.
- **Ecosystem:** proprietary ATRAC + SonicStage + Connect — neither the iTunes side nor the WMA/PlaysForSure side.

## Where the iPod differs

- **Plays the music people actually have.** MP3 + AAC out of the box vs. transcode-everything ATRAC.
- **iTunes vs. SonicStage.** The store and jukebox are the iPod's moat; SonicStage is Sony's anchor.
- **Focus.** One iPod line vs. Sony's parallel, overlapping player families (Network Walkman, VAIO Pocket, PSP-as-media-device talk).

## What to watch

- **Native MP3 support** — persistent rumours that a firmware or the next model (NW-HD3) will finally play MP3 directly. The day that ships, the hardware comparison changes.
- **Connect store fixes** — pricing, catalogue, and whether Sony backs away from ATRAC exclusivity.
- **The 30-hour battery claim** in holiday advertising — it is the one spec where Sony clearly beats the iPod.
