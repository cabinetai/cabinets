# United States

What the Scout knows about renting and buying in the US. Rules differ by state and often by
city: when a rule below decides something for the person, check it for their state and city,
name the source, and say you did. Price ranges are rough starting points only. Live listings,
sold records and the sources named here always win.

## How the market works

- **Bedrooms, not rooms.** A "2 bed, 1 bath" has two bedrooms, a living room, a kitchen and
  one full bathroom. A "1.5 bath" has a half bath (a toilet and a sink). A "studio" has no
  separate bedroom. A "flex" or "convertible" 2 bed is a 1 bed with a wall to add, often
  without a window in the second room.
- **Size** is in square feet. Many New York listings leave it out; ask.
- **Leases** are usually 12 months. Rent is paid monthly, often online. Utilities are often
  extra (electricity, gas, sometimes heat and water); "heat and hot water included" is common
  in older buildings in the Northeast.
- **Approval is a filter.** Landlords check credit, income and references. A common rule is
  income of 40 times the monthly rent in New York City and about 3 times the rent elsewhere.
  Without it, a guarantor (a person, or a guarantor company) is asked for.
- **Move-in money:** usually first month's rent and a security deposit, sometimes last
  month's rent, an application fee, and in some places a broker fee. Know the total before
  you visit.
- **Concessions:** new buildings advertise "net effective" rent (for example "1 month
  free"). The lease rent is higher, and the renewal is priced from the higher number. Always
  compare the gross rent.
- **Good places go fast** in New York, Boston, San Francisco and other tight markets, often in
  days, sometimes in hours. Peak season is late spring to late summer.

## The rules most people ask about

Always check the person's state and city.

- **Security deposits:** many states cap them, often at one or two months' rent. New York
  State caps it at one month's rent and requires it back within 14 days of move-out, with an
  itemised list of any deductions.
- **Application fees:** some states cap them. New York State caps a background and credit
  check fee at 20 dollars.
- **Broker fees in New York City:** since June 2025 (the FARE Act), a broker hired by the
  landlord is paid by the landlord, not the tenant. A tenant pays only a broker they hired
  themselves. An ad that still asks the tenant for a fee for the landlord's broker breaks it.
- **Rent stabilisation:** many older New York City buildings with six or more units have
  rent-stabilised apartments, with capped yearly increases and a right to renew. Ask, and the
  person can check the building on the state housing agency's site (HCR).
- **Habitability:** every state requires a home fit to live in (heat, water, no pests, safe
  wiring). In New York City, landlords must disclose a building's bed bug history and must
  give heat in the cold months.
- **Fair housing:** a landlord may not refuse someone for race, religion, sex, disability,
  family status and other protected traits, and many states and cities add source of income
  (a housing voucher) to the list.

## Buying, in short

Check every figure here with a lender and a real estate attorney or agent before it decides
anything.

- **Down payment:** conventional loans start at 3 to 5 percent for a first home, with private
  mortgage insurance (PMI) until 20 percent equity. FHA loans start at 3.5 percent with a
  credit score of 580 or more. VA loans (for veterans) can be zero down.
- **Pre-approval first.** Sellers in a tight market take only offers with a lender's
  pre-approval letter.
- **Monthly cost** is principal, interest, property tax, insurance (PITI), plus HOA or common
  charges for a condo, or maintenance for a co-op. Lenders like the whole housing payment
  under about 28 percent of gross income, and all debts under about 36 to 43 percent.
- **Closing costs:** usually 2 to 5 percent of the price for a buyer. In New York, add the
  mansion tax on purchases of 1 million dollars or more, paid by the buyer.
- **Co-op or condo (New York):** a co-op sells shares, has a board that approves buyers, often
  asks for 20 percent down or more and for money left in the bank after closing. A condo is
  real property and easier to buy, usually at a higher price.
- **Before you buy:** an inspection, the appraisal, the title search and title insurance, and
  the HOA or building's finances (reserves, pending special assessments, lawsuits).
- **Real sale prices** are public records. Redfin and Zillow show the sold history of a home
  and its neighbours; county assessor and recorder sites have the source.
- **Flood and climate:** check FEMA's flood map for the address. Flood insurance is separate
  from home insurance, and a flood zone can make it required.

## Reading an ad: the words

| Words | Means |
|---|---|
| bd, ba | Bedrooms, bathrooms |
| Walk-up, 4th floor walk-up | No elevator, and which floor |
| Elevator building, doorman | An elevator; a door attendant, often packages kept |
| W/D in unit, laundry in building | A washer and dryer in the apartment; shared machines |
| DW, central air, through-wall AC | Dishwasher; central air conditioning; a wall unit |
| Pre-war, post-war, new development | Built before 1940s; after; a new building |
| No fee, OP (owner pays) | No broker fee for the tenant |
| Net effective, 1 month free | The rent after a concession; the lease rent is higher |
| Rent stabilized | Capped increases and a right to renew (New York) |
| Garden level, lower level | Partly below the street. Damp and dark, usually |
| Flex 2, convertible | A 1 bed where a wall can make a second room |
| Railroad | Rooms in a line, one through the next |
| Utilities included, heat included | What the rent covers |
| HOA, common charges, maintenance | Condo fee; New York condo fee; co-op fee |
| Guarantor, 40x rule | Someone who signs too; income of 40 times the rent |
