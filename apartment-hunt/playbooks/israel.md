# Israel

What the Scout knows about renting and buying in Israel. Rules change: when a number below
decides something for the person, check it at the source named and say you did. Price
ranges here are rough starting points only. Live figures from Madlan, nadlan.gov.il and
the ads you read always win.

## How the market works

- **Rooms.** The living room counts. "3 rooms" is a living room and two bedrooms. A "half
  room" (`חצי חדר`) is a small room, often without a door or a real window.
- **Most rentals are unfurnished,** with a kitchen and often air conditioners, sometimes
  without a fridge or an oven. "Partly furnished" (`מרוהטת חלקית`) usually means a few
  pieces left behind.
- **Leases** are usually 12 months with an option for another year. Rent is often paid with
  12 post-dated cheques or a monthly bank order. The tenant pays `ארנונה` (city tax), `ועד
  בית` (building fee), water, electricity and gas.
- **The good places go in days,** in the centre of the country sometimes in hours. A top
  pick from this morning is worth a call today.
- **Prices are talked down a little,** more often in buying (a few percent) than in
  renting. A rental that sat for a month has room to talk.
- **The safe room (`ממ"ד`)** has been required in new homes since the early 1990s, so a
  building permitted after about 1993 usually has one in every apartment. Older buildings
  have a shared shelter or none. Since 2023 a safe room adds real value and real demand.

## Rough monthly costs beyond the rent

- `ארנונה`: set by each city per square meter and zone. Roughly 300 to 700 ₪ a month for a
  70 meter apartment in the big cities, less in the periphery. Each city publishes its rate
  table ("צו ארנונה").
- `ועד בית`: 100 to 400 ₪ in an ordinary building, 600 to 1,500 ₪ or more in a tower with a
  lobby, a pool or a gym.
- Electricity, water and gas: a few hundred ₪, more in summer with air conditioning.

## The rental law, in short

The Fair Rental Law (an amendment to the Rental and Loan Law, in force since 2017) sets
minimums a lease cannot take away. Check details on gov.il ("שכירות הוגנת").

- **The apartment must be fit to live in** when handed over. A list of faults (no running
  water, no electricity, no lock, mould, a leaking roof and more) makes it unfit. The owner
  must fix faults they are responsible for in a reasonable time: up to 30 days, and up to 3
  days for an urgent one.
- **Guarantees that cost the tenant money are capped:** a cash deposit or a bank guarantee
  is at most the lower of 3 months' rent or a third of the rent for the whole lease. The
  owner returns it within 60 days of the lease's end, minus what the tenant really owes.
  The cap is not read as covering a promissory note (`שטר חוב`) or guarantors, so an owner
  who asks for a larger note is not breaking it.
- **An option to extend has a notice time:** a tenant who uses an option says so at least
  60 days before the end, and an owner at least 90 days. This is about an option written
  in the lease. A lease with no option ends on its date, and nothing makes either side
  give notice that it will not be renewed. Ask the owner early, in writing.
- **The broker's fee:** the tenant pays only for a broker the tenant hired. A broker working
  for the owner is the owner's to pay. Many brokers still ask the tenant to sign a fee
  agreement: read it before signing, and never pay without a signed order (`הזמנת שירותי
  תיווך`). The usual fee is one month's rent plus VAT (18% since 2025).
- **The owner pays** for structural repairs and wear from normal age; **the tenant pays**
  for damage they cause and for ordinary upkeep.

## Buying, in short

Check every figure here before it decides anything: the tax authority's calculator, the
Bank of Israel and a mortgage adviser.

- **Mortgage limits (Bank of Israel):** a buyer of a first and only home can borrow up to
  75 percent of the price, someone selling to upgrade up to 70 percent, an investor buying
  another apartment up to 50 percent. Banks want the monthly payment to stay under about a
  third of net household income. Above 40 percent a bank treats the loan as higher risk
  and prices it so. Above 50 percent a bank may not lend.
- **Purchase tax (`מס רכישה`):** for a single home, nothing up to about 1.98 million ₪, then
  rising brackets (3.5, 5, 8 and 10 percent). For an additional apartment, 8 percent up to
  about 6 million ₪ and 10 percent above. The brackets are updated yearly.
- **Other costs:** a lawyer (about 0.5 to 1 percent plus VAT), a broker (usually 2 percent
  plus VAT, open to talk), an appraiser, a mortgage adviser if used, and moving.
- **Check before signing:** `נסח טאבו` (the land registry extract: who owns it, liens,
  warnings), the building file at the city (permits for every room; an enclosed balcony
  often has none), and the `היטל השבחה` (betterment levy) question for urban renewal.
- **New from a developer:** payments follow construction, protected by a bank guarantee
  for each payment (`חוק המכר`). Ask for it every time. Delays of months are common.
- **Urban renewal:** TAMA 38 and its successor plans add a safe room, an elevator and floors
  to an old building; `פינוי בינוי` replaces the building. For a buyer it can be value; for a
  tenant it means years of building work next door.
- **Real sale prices** are public at nadlan.gov.il, per street, with size, floor and date.
