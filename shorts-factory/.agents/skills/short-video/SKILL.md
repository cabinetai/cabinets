# Short video playbook

A short video is 15 to 40 seconds, vertical, watched with the sound off as often as on. It
wins or loses in its first second, and it sells only when a viewer believes it. Everything
here serves those two facts.

## What an idea is

One true thing about the business, said so a stranger stops scrolling. Never two things.

- **The fact** comes from the brand profile or a page in the cabinet: a number, a habit, a
  promise, a customer story, a process, a price rule. If you cannot point to the page, it is
  not a fact and there is no idea.
- **The angle** is why a stranger would care about that fact, in one line.
- **The hook** is the first words on screen, at most 7 words, readable in one second.

## Hooks that stop the scroll

Pick the pattern that fits the fact. Use a different pattern for each idea in a set.

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The number | A specific number first | "400 croissants before 6am" |
| The contrast | What people expect, then what is true | "Most planners sell packages. We don't." |
| Behind the scenes | The part nobody sees | "What happens after you book" |
| The rule | A promise the business keeps | "Price beaten in 7 days or it's free" |
| The story | One real customer, by first name only if the page names them | "Noa almost cancelled her honeymoon" |
| How it works | A process in steps | "Your trip, planned in 3 calls" |
| The mistake | A common mistake the business fixes | "You're booking flights too early" |
| The question | A question the audience already asks | "Is a travel planner worth it?" |
| The callout | Name the viewer, so the right people stop | "Planning a honeymoon? Watch this first" |

Rules for every hook:

- Concrete beats clever: a number, a name, a time, a place.
- No clickbait that the video does not pay off. The video must deliver the hook in its first
  five seconds.
- No question that can be answered "no" and scrolled past.
- Sentence case. No hashtags, emoji or exclamation marks in the hook.

## Pictures

The pictures decide if a video looks real or looks made by a machine.

- **One shoot.** Every picture shares one look: the same light and colours. A person the
  video follows looks the same in every scene they are in: same face, same clothes. A
  viewer should believe one person filmed it on one day.
- **Real where it fits.** The business's own pictures beat any made picture, in the scene
  whose line is about what they show, and nowhere else. A pop-out is a punchline: one or
  two in a video, on the product, a result or the name. Every video the same way is a
  template, and viewers scroll past templates: change which pictures you use and where.
- **The line decides the shot.** Show what the line means, not only its nouns: "we answer
  in 15 minutes" is a phone lighting up in a hand, not a clock.
- **Change the shot.** Close-up, wide, detail, medium. A new picture every 2 to 4 seconds.
- **Open close.** The first frame is a face, a hand or the product, close and bright.
- **Real, not stock.** Natural light, real places, people doing something, nobody posing.
- **Room for captions.** The subject in the top half; the lower middle stays simple.

## Matching the goal

The profile's goal decides how a video ends.

| Goal | The last line asks for |
|---|---|
| More sales | One clear way to buy or book, named plainly |
| More leads | A free first step: a call, a quote, a question in the comments |
| More followers | A reason to follow: "Part 2 tomorrow", a series name |
| More visits | The place, the hours, the one thing to try first |

## Platforms

- **TikTok:** fastest cut, the hook on screen in the first frame, captions in the centre,
  15 to 30 seconds.
- **Instagram Reels:** the same, a little more polish, 15 to 30 seconds; the caption carries
  the first line of the hook.
- **YouTube Shorts:** up to 40 seconds; a clear title matters because Shorts are searched.
- **X:** under 30 seconds, captions always, the post text says the hook.
- **Facebook:** captions always (most watch muted), slightly slower, warmer.

## Never

- Invent a number, a result, a review, a price or a customer.
- Say anything the profile lists under "What never to say".
- Name a competitor, promise what the business does not promise, or make health, money or
  legal claims the pages do not make.
- Use an em dash or an en dash in anything the person or a viewer reads.
