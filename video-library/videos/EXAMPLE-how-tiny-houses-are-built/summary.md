# How Tiny Houses Are Built, Start to Finish

EXAMPLE: written from a made-up transcript.

## In five lines

- A two-person team builds a 20 square meter house on a trailer in six weeks.
- The main idea: weight decides everything, from the frame to the kitchen counter.
- They frame in light steel, then add windows, wiring and a sheep wool insulation.
- It ends on the costs, and the three things they would change next time.
- For anyone planning a tiny house who wants to see the order of the work.

## Key points

- **0:14** Every choice starts with one question: how much does it weigh?
- **2:01** Light steel framing is about a third lighter than timber for the same strength.
- **4:05** Fewer, bigger windows: every frame adds weight and a place to leak.
- **6:50** Plan every socket on paper before a single cable goes in.
- **9:00** Sheep wool insulation breathes and handles damp better than foam.
- **15:05** About 31,000 euros in materials, and a lot of weekends.
- **15:52** Next time: a bigger door, the loft ladder moved, more storage under the floor.

## Worth quoting

> "A small house isn't a small project. It's every project, squeezed into less room." **(6:12)**

> "Build the smallest version of everything first. You can always add, you can rarely take away." **(17:20)**
