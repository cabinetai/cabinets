# Pricing Your First Product (Without Guessing)

EXAMPLE: written from a made-up transcript.

## In five lines

- A maker explains how to set the first price for something you sell.
- Three ways in: what it costs you, what it is worth to the buyer, and what they pay today.
- Most first prices are too low, and a low price is harder to raise than a high one to lower.
- It ends on a simple test: ask ten people, and raise the price until two say no.
- For anyone about to sell their first product or service.

## Key points

- **0:32** Most first prices are picked to feel safe, and end up too low.
- **2:10** Cost plus gives you the floor, never the ceiling.
- **5:05** Value pricing: what the buyer saves or gains, not what it costs you.
- **7:40** What they pay today is your real competitor, often a spreadsheet.
- **11:45** A low price is harder to raise than a high one is to lower.
- **14:50** Test: ask ten people, raise the price until two say no.

## Worth quoting

> "That's your real competitor. Not the other startup. The spreadsheet." **(8:32)**

> "Your first price isn't forever. It's your first guess, made on purpose." **(21:30)**
